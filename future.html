Pages that glide.

Tap the card and it grows into the next page, instead of a white flash. It is a few lines of CSS with no script.

Limited supportCross-page transitions are not yet in every major browser engine (Baseline: limited, webstatus.dev, 19 November 2026), so we use them as an extra, never a requirement. In the other browsers the page simply loads. If your device asks for less motion, there is no glide at all.

03 / 06

What an AI assistant sees.

Flip the switch. Under every page there is a second version written for machines: the type of business, its name, its services and where it works. This is how search engines and AI assistants read your business. Most small-business sites give them almost nothing.

95 of 478 construction company sites we scanned in November 2026 (20%) named a business type in their structured data.

Works everywhereStructured data (schema.org) is plain text in the page, so every browser carries it, and Microsoft's NLWeb project builds on it. How much a given AI assistant uses it varies, and we promise no rankings.

What's coming.
Where websites are heading, from the nearest to the furthest. Each item says how sure we are. Predictions, not promises. Sources are listed at the bottom.
Likely strong evidence today Possible mixed evidence Speculative little evidence
Now
2026
  • Likely
    Google AI summaries cut clicks: 8% of visits clicked a result when a summary showed, 15% when it did not (Pew Research, 2025).
  • Likely
    Passkeys are mainstream, and Chrome and Safari support the Digital Credentials API.
  • Likely
    WCAG 2.2 AA is the working accessibility standard.
Soon
2027 to 2030
  • Possible
    Australian private-sector Digital ID goes live, and clinics and age-restricted sellers start to use it.
  • Possible
    Booking and quote requests made by AI agents for customers become common.
  • Possible
    Smart glasses read local business details aloud.
  • Speculative
    Pages that rearrange themselves for each visitor, on ordinary business sites.
Later
2031 to 2036
  • Possible
    Most first contact with a local business starts in an assistant, not a search page.
  • Possible
    Browsers show where a photo or video came from, by default.
  • Possible
    Structured, API-style booking replaces many web forms.
Far
2037 to 2046
  • Speculative
    Wearable and ambient assistants become the default front door.
  • Possible
    The web page stays as the trusted record and the fallback.
  • Speculative
    Spatial computing is mainstream for ordinary business sites.
The Far column is our own judgement, with low confidence. We found no sourced predictions that far out.
